# Copyable prompt: English chart

Replace `[PERSON]` with the person you want to chart. An agent that can run code may use this repository's renderer; other agents can follow the same research and layout rules.

```text
Create an English-language infographic titled "If [PERSON]'s Life Were a Stock" and deliver the finished image.

1. Verify the key life events with reliable sources, favoring primary records or authoritative institutions. Separate documented facts, your interpretation, and the conceptual index values. Use only the date precision the sources support.
2. Select meaningful turning points, with 25 events as a ceiling, not a target. A short life or sparse historical record should have substantially fewer. Give each event a concise title, significance, and up/down/neutral direction.
3. Place events on a real chronological x-axis and use a 0–100 conceptual y-axis. Show years only on the chart, followed by age (for example, "1976 · age 21"); use "c." for approximate ages. Do not add a subtitle beneath the main title.
4. Label no more than four life or career periods near the curve. Use green for upward movement, red for downward movement, and gray for neutral stages. These colors describe the chosen narrative angle, not moral approval.
5. Put a readable timeline of every selected event below the chart. Keep all visible text in English, including the title, labels, age notation, event summaries, and disclaimer. Avoid clipped words and overlapping text. If you generate a portrait of a historical person, label it as an artistic interpretation.
6. Do not automatically treat illness, adoption, relationships, death, or other people's suffering as personal failure. You may bring the index to zero at death only if the chart explains that zero marks the end of life, not the loss of achievements or influence.
7. State visibly that the index is a narrative metaphor, not a real stock price, wealth curve, happiness score, or measure of human worth.

Deliver (a) the finished chart, (b) editable event and index data, (c) a short English social post, and (d) source links for the key historical facts. If you cannot render an image, provide complete structured data for a charting tool and say plainly that no image was produced.
```
