# Li Longji / Emperor Xuanzong — research and editorial record

## Scope

This chart has **18 nodes**, deliberately below the 25-node ceiling. The conceptual LONGJI index tracks political authority and the outcomes of imperial governance, not personal happiness, the value of a human life, or the welfare of every Tang subject. Index values and the precise year of the curve's peak are editorial judgments. The An Lushan rebellion was a catastrophe for many people; its decline on this chart is not merely an emperor's personal setback.

The poster displays **years only**. The JSON retains modern conventional birth and death dates (685-09-08 and 762-05-03), and several dates or months of well-documented events, to calculate ages. The accession conversion follows the [Chinese Text Project record](https://ctext.org/datawiki.pl?if=en&res=573182); Yang Guozhong's appointment follows [its linked annal](https://ctext.org/datawiki.pl?if=en&remap=gb&res=150828). The rebellion's date follows the modern conventional conversion of the 755 winter event in the [Palace Museum account](https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/243350.html), corroborated by the [China Grand Canal Museum's year and lunar month](https://m.canalmuseum.org.cn/res/mpwork/be39852bcd974f12962108850c059dfb.pdf). Other nodes use year-only precision, so their displayed ages are approximate. Exact-day conversions from the historical Chinese calendar are not printed on the poster as if they were uncontested contemporary dates. The AI-generated portrait is an artistic interpretation, not an authentic likeness.

## Limits

- The early Kaiyuan era and the Tianbao decline were gradual; choosing 734 as a peak makes a readable editorial arc, not a precise historical measurement.
- The late Tang crisis had structural, military, fiscal, and court-political causes. The chart selects decisions tied to Xuanzong but does not claim one person or one relationship caused the rebellion.
- “Returning to zero” at death is a lifespan convention only. His reign's cultural achievements and the rebellion's consequences continued after 762.
